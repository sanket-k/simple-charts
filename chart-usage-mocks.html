How to Keep Cognitive Load Minimal at 50 Charts

1. Cognitive Psychology (Hick's Law) — Cap Visible Grid at 6–9 Tiles

Showing 50 icons in a 3-column grid creates 17 rows of visual noise and buries Labels and Style below the fold. Keep only Most Used (3) + Core (3 or 6) always visible, and tuck the rest behind a single dropdown.

2. Information Architecture — Organize by Question, Not Geometry

Users think "I want to compare before vs. after", not "I want a Dumbbell chart." Grouping the dropdown by Intent (Trend, Compare, Part-of-Whole, Relationship, Flow, Frameworks) lets users scan 6 folders of ~8 items instead of 50 flat tiles.

3. Data-Driven Discovery — Leverage validateChartData()

Simple Charts already inspects column count, positive-only numbers, and date columns in src/charts/registry.js. Highlighting "Fits your data" surfaces specialized charts (like Dumbbell, Dual-Bar, or Sankey) at the exact moment they succeed.

4. New Chart Discovery — Quiet New Badges + Search (⌘K)

Track seen chart IDs locally and show a quiet 3 New chip on the dropdown button + a New filter pill inside the catalog. Once opened/tried, the badge clears automatically—zero popup fatigue.
